## "No Director, Only Victims"

In this revised version of **Night of the Thirteenth**, we are stripping away the safety net. There is no permanent Game Master to guide you, to save you, or to condemn you. Instead, in each scene the **Active Player (AP)** steps into the spotlight as both the Protagonist and the **Director for the scene**—responsible for framing the start of the scene, maintaining the tone, and holding final authority (and veto power) over their character's actions.

They are not alone. The rest of the table shifts roles to play side characters, the **Camera Crew**, and the **Editor**—answering sensory questions, escalating the danger, and ensuring the horror lands with maximum impact. You are all responsible for the nightmare.

## Requirements

To play this game, you will need a specific set of tools:
*   **A standard playing card deck**: Ensure it has two distinct Jokers (Red and Black, or otherwise distinguishable).
*   **Dice**: At least one pair consisting of a **d4** and a **d10**.
*   **Tokens**: Exactly **13 tokens** to represent the Genre Points. Poker chips, coins, or glass beads work well.
*   **Writing Materials**: Pen and paper for every player to record their name, their archetype, and their inevitable demise.

> **Rule of Thumb**: Generally, more specific rules trump generic rules, and playset instructions supersede core rule instructions. If a card or playset says one thing and the core rules say another, follow the playset.

## The d13

The game uses a unique die roll called the **“d13”**. This is not a single die, but a combination of a **d10** and a **d4**.

*   The **d10 (Main Die)** represents your skill and luck. It is read so that the `0` face represents **zero** (not ten), producing results ranging from **0 to 9**.
*   The **d4 (Effort Die)** represents how hard you are pushing yourself. It produces a result between **1 and 4**.

These two numbers are added together to get a final result between **1 and 13**.

> **Important**: A modifier can never raise or lower a die result outside its natural range (`0–9` for the d10, `1–4` for the d4). You cannot roll higher than 13 or lower than 1.

## The Table Setup

Arrange the table so everyone can see the cards. This is your board, your map, and your doom.

### The Threat Deck
The horror starts small, but it is always there.
**To Build It (Standard Setup)**:
1.  **Separate**: Take the **Face Cards** (Jacks, Queens, Kings), **Aces**, and **Jokers** aside.
2.  **Shuffle Numbers**: Shuffle the remaining Number Cards (`2–10`).
3.  **Deal**: Deal **8 number cards** face down to form the base of the Threat Deck.
4.  **Add Jack**: Add **one random Jack** to this pile and shuffle it again. *(Note: Only Jacks can appear in Act 1).*
5.  **The Prologue**: Place the **four Aces** on top of this stack **face up**.

These four Aces represent the Prologue (Establishing Shots). While they are in play, only one card is active at a time—the top Ace. Once all four Aces are resolved, you will **draw up to two cards** from the top of the Threat Deck and place them face up on the table.

### The Reserves
The remaining cards wait in the wings:
*   **Number Reserve**: Deal **14 of the remaining number cards** face down to form the Number Reserve. *(One will immediately be flipped to start the Trophy Pile, leaving 13 cards in the Reserve. Any leftover number cards are removed from the game without looking at them).*
*   **Face Card Reserves**: Shuffle the remaining **Jacks (3)**, **Queens (4)**, and **Kings (4)** into three separate face-down piles.
*   **The Two Jokers**: Kept separate and face up, watching. They are reserved for the Finale.

### The Trophy Pile
To begin the night with a sense of dread, you must seed the Trophy Pile:
*   **Standard Setup**: Take the **top card** from the **Number Reserve** and place it **face up** next to the Threat Deck. This is your starting Trophy Card (leaving 13 cards in the Number Reserve).

The Trophy Pile moves the story forward. The **Top Card** of this pile sets the **Base Difficulty** for any Test against a **Face Card** or a **Joker**:
*   **Aces & Number Cards (`1–10`)**: Have a static difficulty equal to their **Rank** (`1` for Aces, `2–10` for Number Cards).
*   **Face Cards**: Difficulty = `Trophy Top Rank + Modifier` (**+1** for Jack, **+2** for Queen, **+3** for King).
*   **Jokers**: Difficulty = `Trophy Top Rank + 0`.

As you succeed against Number Cards (`2–10`), you will place them on top of the Trophy Pile, changing the Base Difficulty for the next confrontation with the Killer.

### Optional Rules Modules (Playsets)
Playsets may enable special rule modules that modify setup or gameplay:
*   **Classic Setup (`classicSetup`)**: Used in playsets such as *Generic Slasher (classic)*. Uses the original curated deck setup:
    *   **Number Reserve**: Shuffle `5s`, `6s`, `7s`, `8s`, `9s`, and `10s` separately. Stack them face down so `5s` are on top and `10s` are on the bottom.
    *   **Threat Deck**: Shuffle all `2s`, `3s`, and `4s` together with **1 random Jack** face down. Place the **4 Aces** face up on top.
    *   **Trophy Pile**: Draw the bottom card of the Number Reserve (a random `10`) and place it face up to start the Trophy Pile.
*   **Final Girl Rule (`finalGirl`)**: Used in playsets such as *Summer Camp Massacre*.
    *   Any encounter with the Killer (whenever a Face Card is drawn onto the table) immediately results in **1 Strike** to assign.
    *   **Act 3** and **The Finale** (adding the Jokers) begin immediately when there is **only one surviving character** left.

## Creating the Cast

You are no heroes. You are just in the wrong place at the wrong time. You are the victims in a slasher movie, and the script is already written.

1.  **Choose a Name**: Make it sound like a movie character.
2.  **Come up with a clear Archetype**: Who are you in the group hierarchy?
3.  **Answer “Why are you here?”**: Why didn't you stay home?
4.  **Select an Aptitude**: Each of the four characters claims one distinct Suit (**♠ Power**, **♥ Resolve**, **♣ Intellect**, or **♦ Finesse**). Your Aptitude represents your character's natural strengths.

> **Optional On-the-Fly Creation**: You can create characters as you play out the first four scenes (the Prologue Aces), letting the order of the four Aces determine each player's Aptitude.

### Archetype Examples
*   **♠ Spades (Power — Physical threat)**: The Jock, The Protective, The Townie, The Greaser, The Sheriff, The Father, The Rival
*   **♥ Hearts (Resolve — Fear/Paranoia)**: The Final Them, The Babysitter, The Pure, The Sibling, The Atoner, The New Kid, The Sheriff’s Child
*   **♣ Clubs (Intellect — The Truth)**: The Genre Savant, The Reporter, The Skeptic, The Loner, The Bookworm, The Techie, The Conspiracy Nut
*   **♦ Diamonds (Finesse — Mistakes/Social)**: The Rebel, The Class Clown, The Royal Bee, The Thief, The Rich Kid, The Cheerleader, The Slacker

## Genre Points

The Table starts with **13 Genre Points** in a central pool on the table. These are the currency of the story.

*   **Awarding**: At the end of a scene, any player can award a Genre Point from the table pool to the Active Player (or another player) for playing into a horror trope (*"I'll be right back!"*), making a great roleplay choice, or genuinely terrifying the table.
*   **Spending**: During the Resolution Phase, you can spend one held Genre Point to **reroll both dice (your entire d13)** and add **+1 to the d10 Main Die** (up to a maximum of 9). **Warning**: You must accept the new result, even if it is worse. The spent token is removed from the game entirely.

## The Three Acts & The Finale

The game is played across three Acts, escalating the horror from creeping dread to an explosive finale.

### Act 1: The Setup
The Killer is a presence, a shadow, but not yet acting openly. We get to learn about the characters before the massacre begins in earnest.
*   **Duration**: From the start of the game until the **first confrontation with a Face Card is resolved** (Win or Lose).
*   **Rules**: Play begins with the four Aces (Establishing Shots), followed by drawing two cards at a time from the Threat Deck. When the Jack is revealed and finally resolved (whether you succeed or fail), **Act 2** begins immediately.

### Act 2: The Horror Story
You have faced the Killer, and the horror now escalates. The Killer is actively hunting you.
*   **Duration**: From the resolution of the first Face Card until **four Weaknesses are found** OR **13 cards have been added from the Number Reserve**.
*   **Rules**: Face Cards represent the Killer's direct intervention. Defeating a Face Card of a given Suit (`♠`, `♥`, `♣`, `♦`) for the first time uncovers one of the Killer's four **Weaknesses** and removes that Face Card from the game.

### Act 3: The Climax
This is it—the final confrontation. There is no running anymore.
*   **Trigger**: Begins when **four Weaknesses are found** OR **13 cards have been added from the Number Reserve** (or when only 1 survivor remains if using the *Final Girl Rule*).
*   **Setup**: **Remove all Number Cards** from the Threat Deck (and discard the Number Reserve). Only Face Cards remain—every scene is now a direct confrontation with the Killer.

### The Finale: The Jokers
Should you uncover all four of the Killer's Weaknesses, the true ending begins.
*   **Trigger**: Occurs when **all four Weaknesses are found** (one Face Card from each of the four suits has been defeated), or when triggered by the *Final Girl Rule*.
*   **Setup**: Shuffle both the **Red Joker** and **Black Joker** into the remaining Face Cards in the Threat Deck. Survive until the Red Joker appears, then defeat it to win the game.

## The Game Loop

The game proceeds in scenes. In each scene, one player steps up as the **Active Player (AP)** and acts as the Director for that scene.

### Phase 1: Scene Setup (Choose Your Path)

**The Prologue (Aces — Establishing Shots)**
The game begins with the four Aces on top of the Threat Deck. Each Ace showcases the character with the matching **Aptitude**.
*   Because an Ace has a Difficulty of **1** (`Rank 1`), it is **mechanically impossible to fail** (`d10 + d4` is always at least `1`).
*   Because the character is acting within their own **Aptitude**, they can shift their Effort Die (`d4`) down by `1`—meaning the worst Effort level they can suffer on an Ace is **Overexertion (3)**, never a Breaking Point (4).
*   Once an Ace scene is resolved, **remove the Ace from the game** and place it in front of that player as a reminder token of their Suit/Aptitude. *(Aces do **not** go to the Trophy Pile, and no card is added from the Number Reserve).*

**The Main Game**
Once all four Aces are resolved, the Active Player ensures there are **two cards** drawn face up on the table from the Threat Deck, then chooses **one** to tackle as the current scene. The unchosen card remains on the table for a future scene.
*   **Face Card Lock**: If a Face Card is on the table, you **cannot draw** another card from the Threat Deck while that Face Card remains on the table. If another card was already on the table alongside the Face Card, you may resolve that other card first, leaving the Face Card as the sole remaining card that the next Active Player **must** confront.

### Phase 2: Conversation & Stakes (Focusing the Lens)

The Active Player is the **Director for the scene**, responsible for starting the scene and maintaining the tone. They have complete authority over their character's actions and a **veto right** over anything suggested by the other players (except step 3 below). The other players take the roles of side characters and assist the Active Player as the scene builds toward a risky attempt where success is left to the dice.

1.  **Set the Scene**:
    *   **Active Player**: Based on the scene prompt, describe where this scene is taking place. Your character is front and center.
    *   **Other Players**: Describe the situation and any other characters present in the scene.
2.  **Focus the Camera**:
    *   The **Active Player** asks one of the following Act-specific sensory questions, and the **other players** answer:
        *   **Act 1**: *"What does it look like?"*, *"What does it smell like?"*, *"How close is the Killer?"*, *"Is it pitch black?"*, *"How dark is it?"*
        *   **Act 2**: *"What sound makes you freeze?"*, *"Where is the blood coming from?"*, *"Who is watching you?"*
        *   **Act 3**: *"Why is there no hope?"*, *"What part of your body hurts the most?"*, *"How do you know you're going to die?"*
3.  **Define Goals and Escalate ("Something's Not Right...")**:
    *   Once per scene, any other player can interject: **"Something's not right..."** and add a terrifying detail that makes the situation worse. The Active Player **must accept this truth** (no veto).
4.  **Commit to the Effort (The Stakes)**:
    *   Before rolling, the table asks the Active Player: **"If you end up pushing yourself to reach your goal, what are you willing to risk?"**
    *   Discuss the risk together and agree on a specific **Overexertion** sacrifice that fits the current tension of the story (*Act 1*: risks are not so dire; *Act 2*: stakes are higher; *Act 3*: terrible sacrifices). You may not roll the dice until the table has agreed on what happens if the character Overexerts themselves.

### Phase 3: The Resolution (Rolling the Dice)

The Active Player rolls the **d13 (`d10` + `d4`)** and compares their **Total** to the **Target Difficulty**:
*   **Equal or Greater (`Total >= Difficulty`)**: **Success.** The Active Character achieves their goal and pushes through the challenge.
*   **Less (`Total < Difficulty`)**: **Failure.** The Active Character falls short and the situation spirals out of control.

#### Modifiers & Edge
*   **Spending a Genre Point**: You may spend a held Genre Point to **reroll both dice** and add **+1 to the d10 Main Die** (max 9). You must accept the new result, and the spent Genre Point is removed from the game.
*   **Aptitude (`±1` Effort Die)**: If the Threat Card's Suit matches an **Aptitude** held by a **living character**, you may shift the **Effort Die (`d4`) result up or down by 1** (within the `1–4` range) after rolling:
    *   **Increase Effort (`+1`)**: Push yourself harder to turn a Failure into a Success (increasing your Total by 1), at the cost of a harsher Effort tier.
    *   **Decrease Effort (`-1`)**: Rely on natural talent to reduce the Effort tier (avoiding a Strike or Overexertion), at the risk of lowering your Total by 1.
    *   **Lost Aptitudes & Jokers**: If the character associated with a Suit has died (3 Strikes), that Suit's Aptitude is unavailable for the rest of the game. Furthermore, **no one gets an Aptitude edge against a Joker** during the Finale.

#### Narrative Control: The Effort Scale (Pushing Your Luck)

Regardless of whether you succeeded or failed, the **d4 (Effort Die)** determines the toll of the attempt:

*   **1 — Controlled Effort (*Safe and effective*)**: You kept your cool. You narrate the outcome (Success or Failure) exactly how you want it. You look capable, or your failure is tragic and noble.
*   **2 — Pushing It (*Effort with minor costs*)**: You exerted yourself. You narrate the core outcome. The other players suggest 2–3 potential "costs" (scratches, noise, lost time). You choose one to include.
*   **3 — Overexertion (*Guaranteed sacrifice*)**: You pushed too hard. Whether you Succeeded or Failed, the **Sacrifice** you agreed upon before rolling happens.
    *   *If Success*: You get what you wanted, but you pay the price.
    *   *If Failure*: You don't get what you wanted, and you still pay the price.
*   **4 — Breaking Point (*Disaster strikes*)**: You lost control. The Agreed Sacrifice happens, **AND** it gets worse. The other players suggest **"The Twist"**—a new, horrible piece of information or immediate danger. You choose which Twist becomes true. **(This counts as 1 Strike).**

### Phase 4: Fallout (Updating the Game State)

After narrating the close of the scene, check if a **Genre Point** should be awarded from the table pool, assign any **Strikes** earned during the scene, and update the physical decks:

#### 1. Aces (Prologue)
*   **On Completion**: Remove the Ace from the game (the Active Player keeps it as their Aptitude reminder token). Do **not** add it to the Trophy Pile, and do **not** add a card from the Number Reserve.

#### 2. Number Cards (`2–10`)
*   **On a Success**:
    1. Move the resolved Threat Card to the **top of the Trophy Pile** face up (this becomes the new Base Difficulty for the next Face Card or Joker).
    2. Take the next card from the **Number Reserve** and place it face down at the **bottom of the Threat Deck**.
*   **On a Failure**:
    1. Place the resolved Threat Card face down at the **bottom of the Threat Deck** to haunt you again later.
    2. Take the next card from the **Number Reserve** and place it face down at the **bottom of the Threat Deck**.

*(Note: Once **13 cards** have been added from the Number Reserve to the Threat Deck, **Act 3** triggers immediately at the start of the next scene).*

#### 3. Face Cards (Facing the Killer)
*   **On a Success**:
    1. **Check for Weakness**:
        *   **First Time Defeating This Suit (`♠`, `♥`, `♣`, `♦`)**: **Weakness Found!** Remove this Face Card from the game permanently. *(Finding all 4 Suit Weaknesses triggers **Act 3** and **The Finale**).*
        *   **Weakness Already Known**: You have already found the Weakness for this Suit. This Face Card remains in the Threat Deck.
    2. **The Killer Retaliates** (based on your final **Effort Die `d4`**):
        *   **Effort 1–2 (Controlled / Pushing It)**: Add a random **Jack** from the Face Card Reserves to the Threat Deck.
        *   **Effort 3–4 (Overexertion / Breaking Point)**: Add a random **Queen** from the Face Card Reserves to the Threat Deck.
    3. **The Threat Remains**: Shuffle the **entire Threat Deck** (including any unchosen card on the table) and shuffle the **Trophy Pile** (revealing a new random top Trophy Card).
*   **On a Failure**:
    1. **The Killer Strikes**: The Active Player takes **1 Strike** (in addition to any Strike from rolling a 4 on the Effort Scale).
    2. **The Horror Grows**: Add a random **King** from the Face Card Reserves to the Threat Deck.
    3. **The Threat Remains**: Return the Face Card (and any other visible card on the table) to the Threat Deck, then shuffle the **entire Threat Deck** and shuffle the **Trophy Pile**.

*(Note: Resolving your very first Face Card confrontation—whether you succeed or fail—immediately transitions the game from **Act 1** to **Act 2**).*

**Running Out of Face Card Reserves**:
*   **No Jacks left?** Add a **Queen** instead (or a **King** if no Queens remain).
*   **No Queens left?** Add a **King** instead (or a **Jack** if no Kings remain).
*   **No Kings left?** Add a **Queen** instead (or a **Jack** if no Queens remain).
*   **No Face Cards left at all?** Do not add any card.

#### 4. Jokers (The Finale)
When a Joker is drawn in the Finale, its test uses the **Trophy Pile's top card** (`+0`) as its Target Difficulty, and **no Aptitude modifier** may be used.
*   **The Red Joker (The Final Test)**:
    *   **Success**: **Victory!** The Red Joker is defeated. Any surviving characters see the dawn and win the game.
    *   **Failure**: **The End.** Your character is killed immediately. Shuffle the Red Joker back into the Threat Deck (and shuffle the Trophy Pile); the nightmare continues for any remaining survivors.
*   **The Black Joker (The Twist)**:
    *   **Success**: Remove the Black Joker from the game, and **remove the highest-ranking Face Card** currently in the Threat Deck (`King` $\rightarrow$ `Queen` $\rightarrow$ `Jack`). Shuffle the Threat Deck and Trophy Pile.
    *   **Failure**: Remove the Black Joker from the game, and **add a random King** from the Face Card Reserves to the Threat Deck. Shuffle the Threat Deck and Trophy Pile.

## Three Strikes and You’re Out!

Every character in **Night of the Thirteenth** gets two close calls with death before the end comes for them.

**You gain a Strike when you:**
*   **Fail** any Test against a **Face Card** (the Killer).
*   Roll a **4 (Breaking Point)** on the Effort Scale (whether you Succeed or Fail).
*   *(If playing with the **Final Girl Rule** module)*: Whenever a **Face Card is drawn** onto the table.

When a character receives their **third Strike**, their story is over in a genre-fitting, gruesome fashion:
*   That character is **eliminated**, and their **Suit Aptitude** is permanently lost for the remainder of the game.
*   If **all four characters** are killed, the Killer wins and the game ends in defeat.
