# Night of the Thirteenth v2.0 — Complete Game Rules

**(c) 2025 Gogam — Designed by Petri Leinonen — Revised Interaction Core by The Table**

---

## 1. Concept & Premise

Night of the Thirteenth is a **Director-less horror TTRPG** for 4 players. You are not heroes — you are victims in a slasher movie, and the script is already written. There is no Game Master; instead, the table collaborates to build the horror.

### "No Director, Only Victims"

The **Active Player (AP)** takes the spotlight as the Protagonist of the current scene, driving the action and making the hard choices. The rest of the table shifts roles to become the **Camera Crew and the Editor** — framing the shot, cutting to the reaction, and ensuring the horror lands with maximum impact. You are all responsible for the nightmare.

### The Golden Rule

> **Specificity Trumps Generality.** If a card says one thing and the book says another, follow the card. Playset instructions supersede core rule instructions.

## 2. Requirements

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Playing Card Deck** | A standard 52-card deck plus **two distinct Jokers** (Red and Black, or otherwise distinguishable). |
| **Dice** | At least one **d10** and one **d4**. |
| **Tokens** | Exactly **13 tokens** for Genre Points (poker chips, coins, glass beads). |
| **Writing Materials** | Pen and paper for every player — for names, archetypes, and inevitable obituaries. |

## 3. The d13

The game uses a unique die roll called the **d13**. It is not a single die, but a combination:

| Die | Name | Role | Range |
|-----|------|------|-------|
| **d10** | Main Die | Represents skill and luck | **0–9** (the "0" face reads as zero, not ten) |
| **d4** | Effort Die | Represents how hard you push yourself | **1–4** |

**Total = d10 + d4**, producing a result between **1 and 13**.

> **Hard Cap:** A modifier can never raise the result above 13 or below 1. The d13 is bounded.

### Example

> You roll the d10 and get a 6. You roll the d4 and get a 3. Your Total is **9**.

## 4. Setting Up the Table

The table layout represents the physical board state. This section describes how to build the decks from a standard 52-card deck + 2 Jokers.

### 4.1 Separating the Deck

1. **Set aside** all Face Cards (Jacks, Queens, Kings), all four Aces, and both Jokers.
2. You are left with **36 Number Cards** (2–10, four of each suit).

### 4.2 Building the Threat Deck

The Threat Deck is the engine of horror.

1. Shuffle the **36 Number Cards**.
2. Deal **8 Number Cards** face down. These form the base of the Threat Deck.
3. Add **one Jack** from the reserves to this pile. Shuffle the 9 cards together.
4. Place the **four Aces** face up on top of this stack. Only the top Ace is visible.

> **Note:** Only Jacks can appear as the Killer in Act 1.

#### Optional: Fast Game Mode

For a faster, more intense game, remove one random card of each number rank (2–10) before building the Threat Deck. This reduces the deck by 9 cards.

### 4.3 The Reserves

The remaining cards are not yet in play:

| Reserve | Contents |
|---------|----------|
| **Number Reserve** | The remaining **14 Number Cards** (face down). One will move to the Trophy Pile during setup. |
| **Face Card Reserves** | The remaining **3 Jacks**, **4 Queens**, and **4 Kings** — sorted into separate piles. |
| **Jokers** | The **Red Joker** and **Black Joker** — kept separate and face up. They enter play only during The Finale. |

### 4.4 The Trophy Pile

The Trophy Pile tracks the escalating danger. Its top card determines the difficulty of Killer encounters.

**Setup:** Take the **top card** from the Number Reserve and place it **face up** next to the Threat Deck. This is your starting Trophy Card.

> **Classic Setup Module:** If using the Classic Setup rules module, the Trophy Pile starts with a specific random 10 (e.g., 10 of Spades), and the deck composition is curated rather than randomized. See [Rules Modules](#12-rules-modules) for details.

### 4.5 Initial Counts Summary

| Location | Count | Contents |
|----------|-------|----------|
| Threat Deck (hidden) | 9 cards | 8 number cards + 1 Jack (shuffled) |
| Threat Deck (visible) | 4 cards | 4 Aces (on top, face up) |
| Number Reserve | 13 cards | Remaining number cards |
| Face Card Reserves | 11 cards | 3 Jacks + 4 Queens + 4 Kings |
| Jokers | 2 cards | Red + Black |
| Trophy Pile | 1 card | Top of Number Reserve |
| **Total** | **40 cards** | (12 removed by fast game, if applicable) |

## 5. Creating the Cast

### 5.1 Character Creation Steps

1. **Choose a Name** — Make it sound like a movie character.
2. **Come up with a clear Archetype** — Who are you in the high school hierarchy?
3. **Think of a reason "Why are you here?"** — Why didn't you stay home?
4. **Select an Aptitude** — Choose one suit. This represents your character's natural strengths.

### 5.2 Aptitudes

Each Aptitude corresponds to a card suit. When you face a challenge that matches your Aptitude, you can modify your Effort Die after rolling.

| Suit | Aptitude | Theme | Description |
|------|----------|-------|-------------|
| ♠ Spades | **Power** | Physical threat | Brawn, violence, endurance |
| ♥ Hearts | **Resolve** | Fear/Paranoia | Willpower, empathy, emotional resilience |
| ♣ Clubs | **Intellect** | The Truth | Investigation, puzzles, knowledge |
| ♦ Diamonds | **Finesse** | Mistakes/Social | Stealth, dexterity, social manipulation |

### 5.3 Archetype Examples

| Suit | Archetypes |
|------|------------|
| ♠ Power | The Jock, The Protective, The Townie, The Greaser, The Sheriff, The Father, The Rival |
| ♥ Resolve | The Final Them, The Babysitter, The Pure, The Sibling, The Atoner, The New Kid, The Sheriff's Child |
| ♣ Intellect | The Genre Savant, The Reporter, The Skeptic, The Loner, The Bookworm, The Techie, The Conspiracy Nut |
| ♦ Finesse | The Rebel, The Class Clown, The Royal Bee, The Thief, The Rich Kid, The Cheerleader, The Slacker |

### 5.4 Default Characters (Web Assistant)

The web assistant initializes four characters by default, one per suit:

| ID | Default Name | Aptitude |
|----|-------------|----------|

| Hearts | The Resolve | ♥ Resolve |
| Clubs | The Intellect | ♣ Intellect |

## 6. Genre Points

The table starts with exactly **13 Genre Points** in a central pool. They are a finite, non-renewable resource.

### 6.1 Awarding

Any player can award another player a Genre Point for:
- Playing into a horror trope ("I'll be right back!")
- Making a great roleplay choice
- Genuinely terrifying the table

A Genre Point moves from the **table pool** to the **player's personal pool**. The web assistant tracks this at the end of each scene.

### 6.2 Spending

You can spend **one Genre Point** to reroll your entire d13 (both dice). When you do:
- Add **+1** to the new d10 (Main Die) result.
- You **must accept** the second result, even if it's worse.
- The spent Genre Point is **removed from the game entirely** (not returned to the table pool).

### 6.3 Edge Cases

- Genre Points cannot be spent before rolling — only after seeing the result.
- Only one Genre Point can be spent per roll.
- Once spent, the second result is final regardless of outcome.
- The +1 modifier on the d10 still cannot push the d13 total above 13.

## 7. The Three Acts

The game is structured into three escalating Acts.

### 7.1 Act 1: The Setup

The Killer is a presence — a shadow — but not acting directly. Players learn about their characters without an axe-wielding maniac in every scene.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Duration** | From game start until the **first resolution of a Face Card** (win or lose). |
| **Key Rule** | Only Jacks can appear as the Killer. Merely revealing a Jack does not end Act 1 — a player must **resolve** the scene against it. |
| **Prologue** | The game begins with the four Aces (establishing shots for each character). |

### 7.2 Act 2: The Horror Story

The Killer is now part of the story. This is the actual horror movie. People will die.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Duration** | From the resolution of the first Face Card until **four weaknesses are found** OR **13 cards have been added from the Reserve**. |
| **Key Rules** | Queens are added to the mix via Fallout mechanics. The Killer is actively hunting. |

### 7.3 Act 3: The Climax

The explosive climax of the horror movie. The mask comes off, or the monster grows to full size.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Trigger** | **Four weaknesses are found** OR **13 cards have been added from the Reserve**. |
| **Setup** | Remove **all Number Cards** from the Threat Deck. Only Face Cards remain. |
| **Key Rule** | Every encounter is now with the Killer. |

### 7.4 The Finale

The true ending. Triggered when **all four weaknesses** (one per suit) are found.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Setup** | Both **Jokers** are shuffled into the Threat Deck. |
| **Note** | The Finale can trigger simultaneously with Act 3 (if the 4th weakness is found while still in Act 2). |

### 7.5 Act Transition Edge Cases

- **Simultaneous Triggers:** If the 4th weakness is found while still in Act 2, both Act 3 and The Finale trigger at once. The web assistant queues these as sequential setup screens.
- **Act 1 → Act 2:** Transition occurs specifically when a Face Card is **resolved** (success or failure). Drawing a Face Card alone doesn't trigger the transition. If the player fails, they still transition to Act 2 — the Killer has made their presence known.
- **Reserve Counter:** Cards added from the reserve are tracked globally. This counter persists across acts and never resets.

## 8. The Game Loop

Each scene follows a structured loop. One player is the **Active Player (AP)** for the scene.

### 8.1 Scene Setup: Choose Your Path

#### The Prologue (Aces)

When an Ace is visible on top of the Threat Deck, there is no choice. The story demands a specific victim:
- The player whose **Aptitude matches the Ace's suit** must be the Active Player.
- Aces are **establishing shots** — they introduce the character.
- Only one Ace is visible at a time (the top of the pile).
- Aces have **difficulty 1**. Since the minimum d13 roll is 1 (d10=0 + d4=1), Aces **always succeed**. There is no failure case.
- On success, the Ace is **removed from the game** — it becomes that player's **turn order token** (see §8.1.1).

#### The Main Game

Once the Aces are gone, the table should have **two visible cards** at the start of each scene. The unchosen card from the previous scene typically carries over.

**Scene Setup Sub-Flow:**

1. **Ensure at least one card is visible.** If the table is empty (first scene after the Prologue, or after a Face Card resolution that shuffled/cleared the table), draw one card from the Threat Deck.

2. **Select the Active Player.** With only one card visible, the group decides who will face this scene's challenge — a **half-blind** decision, since they can only see one of the (up to) two threat cards. Follow the turn order rules (§8.1.1).

3. **Draw to fill the table (if applicable):**
   - If the visible card is a **number card** (rank 2–10): draw a second card from the Threat Deck to give the AP a choice.
   - If the visible card is a **Face Card, Ace, or Joker**: do **not** draw a second card. The AP must challenge this card immediately — the Killer demands attention.

4. **Choose the challenge.** If two cards are visible, the Active Player selects one to challenge. The unchosen card remains on the table for the next scene.

**Face Card Exception:** If a Face Card is visible on the table, it **must** be chosen. The Killer demands your attention.

**Draw Exception:** If the unchosen card remaining from the previous scene is a Face Card, do **not** draw a replacement. You must deal with that Face Card next.

### 8.1.1 Turn Order (Ace Tokens)

After the Prologue, each player's resolved Ace becomes their **turn order token**. This system ensures every player gets equal screen time.

#### How It Works

1. After the Prologue, each player holds their Ace **face-up** in front of them.
2. At the start of each scene, the group decides who will be the Active Player from among players whose Aces are **face-up** (i.e., who haven't acted yet this round).
3. When a player completes their scene as the Active Player, they **flip their Ace face-down**.
4. A player with a face-down Ace **cannot** be the Active Player until the round resets.
5. Once **all living players** have acted (all Aces are face-down), the round resets — all Aces flip **face-up** again, and a new round begins.

#### Edge Cases

- **Dead characters:** A dead player's Ace is permanently face-down. They are skipped in the turn order. If a character dies mid-round, the round continues with the remaining living players.
- **One player alive:** That player is always the Active Player (no turn order needed — see Final Girl rules module).
- **Prologue overlap:** The turn order system only activates after all 4 Aces have been resolved. During the Prologue, the Ace suit dictates the Active Player.

#### The Prompt

Once a card is selected, look at the **Suit and Rank** to determine the scene prompt.

### 8.2 The Prompt Matrix

The selected card generates a narrative prompt based on suit (theme) and rank (intensity).

#### Number Cards

| Suit | Theme | Low Intensity (2–5) | High Intensity (6–10) |
|------|-------|---------------------|----------------------|
| ♠ Spades | Power | "What physical barrier or trap stops you from moving forward?" | "Something hurts you immediately. What is it, and why can't you fight back?" |
| ♥ Hearts | Resolve | "What creepy detail do you notice that you are too afraid to tell the others?" | "You feel isolated. Why does the person next to you suddenly feel like a threat?" |
| ♣ Clubs | Intellect | "You find a clue. What does it reveal about how the previous victims died?" | "You realize the plan won't work. What crucial resource is missing or broken?" |
| ♦ Diamonds | Finesse | "You trip or fumble. What noise do you make that echoes too loudly?" | "Your ego gets in the way. What stupid decision do you make to prove yourself?" |

#### Face Cards (The Killer)

The prompt is always the same:
> *"The Killer is here. How do they corner you, and what makes them look unstoppable?"*

Face cards also have **firstTime** and **recurring** sub-prompts that describe aspects of the Killer's appearance (silhouette, weapon, face, movement, etc.), depending on whether it's the first encounter with that suit's face card.

#### Aces (Establishing Shots)

Each Ace has a unique establishing shot prompt per suit, e.g.:
- ♠: "We see you handling a physical task with ease. What is it?"
- ♥: "We see you caring for someone or something. Who is it?"
- ♣: "We see you preparing for what's to come. What supply or exit are you checking?"
- ♦: "We see you at the center of attention. What are you saying or doing?"

### 8.3 The Conversation: Focusing the Lens

Before any dice are rolled, the table uses two conversation moves:

#### A. Focus the Camera (Take Stock)

Any player can ask the Active Player a **sensory question** to clarify the scene:
- "What does it smell like in here?"
- "How close is the Killer standing?"
- "Is it pitch black, or is there flickering light?"

#### B. "Something's Not Right" (Escalate)

**Once per scene**, any other player can interject with the phrase *"Something's not right..."* and add a terrifying detail that makes the situation worse. The Active Player **must accept this truth**.

> **Example:**
> - AP: "I try to hotwire the car."
> - Other Player: "Something's not right... there is no ignition. The wires have been ripped out entirely."

### 8.4 The Stakes: Loading the Gun

The table examines the Difficulty Number and asks the Active Player:

> **"If you push yourself, what are you willing to Sacrifice?"**

The Active Player (with help from the table) must define the **Overexertion result**. This must be:
- **Specific** — not vague hand-waving.
- **A genuine loss** — injury, an item destroyed, a relationship damaged.

> **Example:** "If I push this hard, I don't just miss the jump; I twist my ankle and drop the flashlight into the ravine."

### 8.5 The Resolution: The Director's Edit

The Active Player rolls the **d13** (d10 + d4).

#### Success or Failure

Compare your Total to the Difficulty:
- **Total ≥ Difficulty** → **Success.** You overcome the challenge.
- **Total < Difficulty** → **Failure.** The challenge overcomes you.

### 8.6 The Effort Scale (Pushing Your Luck)

Regardless of success or failure, the **d4 result** (Effort Die) determines the narrative cost:

| d4 | Level | Effect |
|----|-------|--------|
| **1** | Controlled Effort | You kept your cool. You narrate the outcome exactly how you want. Success is slick; failure is noble and tragic. **No extra cost.** |
| **2** | Pushing It | You exerted yourself. You narrate the core outcome. Other players suggest 2–3 potential "costs" (scratches, noise, lost time). You choose one. **Minor cost.** |
| **3** | Overexertion | You pushed too hard. The **Sacrifice** (defined in Stakes) happens regardless of success or failure. On success: you get what you wanted but pay the price. On failure: you don't get what you wanted, and still pay. **Guaranteed sacrifice.** |
| **4** | Breaking Point | You lost control. The Sacrifice happens, **AND** it gets worse. Other players suggest "The Twist" — a new, horrible danger. You choose which Twist becomes true. **This always counts as a Strike.** |

### 8.7 Aptitude: Managing the Risk

If your Aptitude matches the Threat Card's suit, you are naturally skilled in this area. After rolling, you may **modify your Effort Die (d4) by +1 or -1**:

| Modification | Effect | Risk |
|-------------|--------|------|
| **Increase (+1)** | Higher Total (helps turn Failure → Success) | Higher Effort level (more sacrifice) |
| **Decrease (-1)** | Lower Effort level (less sacrifice) | Lower Total (risks turning Success → Failure) |

> **Example:** You roll d10=5, d4=2 against a 7 of Spades. Your Total is 7 (success!). Your Aptitude is Spades. You could decrease the d4 to 1 (Controlled Effort) but your Total drops to 6 (failure). Or you keep it as-is: Pushing It, success.

### 8.8 Worked Example: A Complete Scene

> **Setup:** Act 2. Two cards visible: **6 of Hearts** and **Jack of Clubs**. The Face Card (Jack) must be chosen.
>
> **Prompt:** "The Killer is here. How do they corner you, and what makes them look unstoppable?" (Face Card prompt). Sub-prompt (first time Clubs): "What are they wearing that looks out of place?"
>
> **Difficulty:** Trophy Pile top card is a **5**. Jack modifier is +1. Difficulty = **6**.
>
> **AP (The Bookworm, ♣ Intellect):** "I see the Killer blocking the library door, wearing a child's birthday hat… covered in blood. I try to smash through the window."
>
> **Conversation:** Another player: "Something's not right… the window is actually plexiglass."
>
> **Stakes:** "If I push too hard, the glass doesn't break — it bounces me back, and the Killer catches me by the arm."
>
> **Roll:** d10 = 4, d4 = 3. Total = 7. Difficulty = 6. **Success!** But Effort = 3 (Overexertion).
>
> **Aptitude Check:** Clubs matches Clubs! The AP can modify the d4. They choose to decrease it: d4 becomes 2 (Pushing It). New Total = 6. Still ≥ 6. **Still success!** And now the Effort is only "Pushing It" instead of "Overexertion."
>
> **Narration:** The AP narrates crashing through the window. Other players suggest costs: "You cut your hand badly" or "The Killer sees which direction you ran." AP chooses "The Killer sees which direction you ran."
>
> **Fallout:** Success against Face Card. First time defeating ♣ Clubs → **Weakness found!** The Jack is removed from the game. A new Jack is added from reserves (Effort 1-2 = Jack). Threat Deck and Trophy Pile are shuffled.

## 9. Updating the Game State (Fallout)

After narration, update the decks based on the outcome.

### 9.1 Difficulty Calculation

| Card Type | Difficulty |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Number Card (2-10)** | Equal to the card's **Rank** |
| **Ace** | Rank **1** |
| **Jack** | Trophy Pile top card + **1** |
| **Queen** | Trophy Pile top card + **2** |
| **King** | Trophy Pile top card + **3** |
| **Joker (Red or Black)** | Trophy Pile top card + **0** (shuffle Trophy Pile first) |

### 9.2 Number Card Resolution

#### On Success
1. Move the Threat Card to the **Trophy Pile** (it becomes the new top card, setting the new Base Difficulty for Killer encounters).
2. Add the next card from the **Number Reserve** to the bottom of the Threat Deck.

#### On Failure
1. Move the Threat Card to the **bottom of the Threat Deck** (it will return to haunt you).
2. Add the next card from the **Number Reserve** to the bottom of the Threat Deck.

> **Note:** Aces (Rank 1) are **not** resolved through §9.2. They always succeed (difficulty 1, minimum roll 1), and on success they are **removed from the game entirely** — they become the player's turn order token (see §8.1.1). Aces do **not** go to the Trophy Pile, and no Reserve card is drawn after an Ace resolution.

### 9.3 Face Card Resolution (The Killer)

#### On Success

**1. Weakness Check:**
- If this is the **first time** a Face Card of its suit (♠, ♥, ♣, ♦) has been defeated: **Weakness Found!** The card is **removed from the game entirely.**
- If a card of that suit was already defeated: the card **remains in the Threat Deck** (shuffled back in).

**2. Reinforcements (The Killer Learns):**
Based on the Effort Die (d4), a new Face Card is added from reserves:

| Effort Level | Addition |
|-------------|----------|
| 1 or 2 (Controlled / Pushing It) | Add a **Jack** from reserves |
| 3 or 4 (Overexertion / Breaking Point) | Add a **Queen** from reserves |

**Reserve Fallback Chain:**
- No Jacks available? → Add a **Queen** instead.
- No Queens available? → Add a **King** instead.
- No Kings available? → Add a **Jack** or **Queen** instead (whichever is available).
- No Face Cards at all? → Do not add any card.

**3. Shuffle:**
After **any** Face Card confrontation (success or failure), shuffle the **entire Threat Deck** and the **Trophy Pile**. Reveal the new Trophy Pile top card.

#### On Failure

1. **Strike:** The Active Player gains **one Strike**.
2. **The Horror Grows:** Add a **King** from the Face Card Reserves to the bottom of the Threat Deck.
3. **The card remains in the Threat Deck** (shuffled back in).
4. **Shuffle** the Threat Deck and Trophy Pile.

#### Act Transition on Face Card Failure

If a player **fails** against a Face Card while in **Act 1**, the game immediately transitions to **Act 2**. The Killer has made their presence unmistakably known.

### 9.4 Replenishing the Table

After resolving a card:

1. **Draw Replacement:** Draw the top card from the Threat Deck so there are two visible cards again.
2. **Face Card Exception:** If the **unchosen** card remaining on the table is a Face Card, do **not** draw a replacement. You must deal with that Face Card next.
3. **Shuffle Rule:** If a shuffle was triggered (Face Card resolution), shuffle all visible cards back into the deck, then draw two new ones.

### 9.5 Edge Cases — Card Resolution

- **Breaking Point (d4 = 4) on a Face Card success:** The player earns a Strike from Breaking Point **and** the weakness check still applies. Two penalties can stack: the Sacrifice, the Twist, AND a Strike from both Breaking Point and (potentially) a simultaneous failure condition.
- **Breaking Point on a Number Card:** The player earns a Strike from Breaking Point even on success or failure of a number card.
- **Empty Number Reserve:** When the Number Reserve runs out, no card is added to the Threat Deck on number card resolution. The reserve counter still increments for Act 3 trigger purposes.
- **Trophy Pile with multiple cards:** Only the **top** card matters for difficulty. After a shuffle, a random card from the pile becomes the new top.

## 10. Strikes

### 10.1 Strike Triggers

You receive a Strike when:
1. You **fail any Test against the Killer** (a Face Card).
2. You roll a **4 (Breaking Point)** on the Effort Die — regardless of success or failure, and regardless of card type.

> **Warning:** These can stack. Failing a Face Card test with a Breaking Point roll earns **two Strikes** in a single scene.

### 10.2 Three Strikes Rule

When a character reaches their **third Strike**, they die immediately. Come up with the most horrifying, genre-fitting death possible.

### 10.3 Total Party Kill

If all characters are dead, the game ends in defeat. The Killer wins.

## 11. The Jokers (The Finale)

When all four weaknesses are found, both Jokers are shuffled into the Threat Deck.

### 11.1 Joker Activation

When a Joker is **revealed** (drawn as a visible card), its effect triggers **immediately**. Before resolving:

1. **Shuffle the Trophy Pile.**
2. The new top card's rank becomes the **Difficulty** (Joker difficulty = Trophy Top + 0).

### 11.2 Red Joker — "The End"

This is the Final Boss. The true finale. One character must make a final Test.

| Outcome | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **Success** | You've done it. You see the dawn. **All surviving characters win. Game Over.** |
| **Failure** | The character making the Test is **killed**. The Red Joker is **shuffled back** into the Threat Deck. The nightmare continues for remaining survivors. |

### 11.3 Black Joker — "The Twist"

One last desperate hurdle before the end.

| Outcome | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **Success** | Remove the **highest-ranked Face Card** from the Threat Deck (King > Queen > Jack). |
| **Failure** | Add another **King** from reserves to the Threat Deck. |

After resolution (success or failure), the Black Joker is **removed from the game**. Shuffle the Threat Deck and Trophy Pile.

### 11.4 Joker Edge Cases

- **Both Jokers drawn simultaneously:** Process them one at a time. The Black Joker is typically resolved first (it's "The Twist" — the false ending before the real one).
- **Red Joker failure with last survivor:** The last character dies and the game ends in defeat. The Red Joker is not shuffled back because there's no one left to fight.
- **Black Joker success with no Face Cards in deck:** No card is removed. The Black Joker is still removed from the game.

## 12. Rules Modules

Rules Modules are optional variations that modify core game behavior. They are configured per playset.

### 12.1 Classic Setup

Uses the original curated deck composition instead of randomized setup:

| Component | Classic Composition |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Threat Deck** | All 2s, 3s, 4s (12 cards) + 1 Jack |
| **Number Reserve** | All 5s, 6s, 7s, 8s, 9s, 10s |
| **Trophy Pile** | Starts with a specific random 10 |

In Classic mode, the deck composition is known (specific card counts per rank), allowing for more strategic play. In Random mode (default), the initial 8-card deal from a shuffled deck means players don't know exactly which number cards are in the Threat Deck vs. the Reserve.

### 12.2 Final Girl

A special rule for heightened drama:

- **Any encounter with the Killer** (drawing or facing a Face Card) results in a Strike.
- **Act 3 begins immediately** when only one character remains alive.
- The Jokers are added when Act 3 begins under this rule.

This module dramatically increases lethality and accelerates the endgame.

## 13. Complete Turn Reference

### Quick Reference: The Scene Loop

```
1. SCENE SETUP
   └─ Prologue? → Aptitude-matched player takes the Ace
   └─ Main Game? → Choose one of two visible cards
   └─ Face Card visible? → Must choose it

2. THE CONVERSATION
   └─ Focus the Camera (sensory questions)
   └─ "Something's Not Right" (escalate, once per scene)

3. THE STAKES
   └─ Define the Sacrifice (Overexertion result)

4. THE RESOLUTION
   └─ Roll d13 (d10 + d4)
   └─ Compare Total vs. Difficulty
   └─ Aptitude match? → Modify d4 by ±1
   └─ Genre Point? → Reroll + d10 modifier +1

5. NARRATION
   └─ Success/Failure + Effort Scale
   └─ Controlled (1) / Pushing It (2) / Overexertion (3) / Breaking Point (4)

6. FALLOUT
   └─ Update decks (Trophy Pile / Returns / Reserves)
   └─ Check for Weakness discovery
   └─ Check for Act transitions
   └─ Assign Strikes (Breaking Point, Face Card failure)
   └─ Award Genre Points
   └─ Draw replacement cards → Next scene
```

### Difficulty Quick Reference

```
Number Cards:  Difficulty = Rank
Aces:          Difficulty = 1
Jack:          Difficulty = Trophy Top + 1
Queen:         Difficulty = Trophy Top + 2
King:          Difficulty = Trophy Top + 3
Joker:         Difficulty = Trophy Top + 0 (shuffle Trophy first)
```

### Strike Quick Reference

```
Strike triggers:
  ✗ Fail a Face Card test
  ✗ Roll Breaking Point (d4 = 4) on ANY card

3 Strikes = Death
All dead = Game Over (Killer wins)
```
